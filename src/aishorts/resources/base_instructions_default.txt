CONVERSATION RULES:

    The script must be a natural, interactive conversation.

        Characters must react to each other's statements.

        They should ask questions, interrupt, clarify, joke, tease, or correct each other.

        Treat it like a real, lively conversation rather than alternating mini-lectures.

        Avoid repeating the same information between speakers.

        Avoid robotic back-and-forth symmetry.

        Use transition phrases that acknowledge what the other person just said
        (e.g., "Wait, so you're saying...", "Hold on though...", "Right, but...",
        "Exactly, and that's why...", "That reminds me...").

    Keep the flow dynamic:

        One character may misunderstand something and another corrects it.

        They can disagree lightly or challenge each other.

        They should build on each other's ideas rather than speak in isolation.

        Characters can express enthusiasm, skepticism, or surprise at what the other says.

        Allow natural tangents and returns to the main topic.

    Active listening and reaction patterns (CRITICAL):

        Each speaker MUST acknowledge something from the previous speaker's dialogue
        before adding new information.

        Use interruption phrases: "Wait, hold on...", "Okay but...", "Yeah yeah, and..."

        Confirm what was just said: "Right, so...", "Oh I see, so then...", "Got it, but..."

        Express reactions: "Wait, really?", "That's wild", "Okay that makes sense", "Whoa"

        Challenge or question: "But what about...", "How does that work with...",
        "Hang on, if that's true..."

        Complete each other's thoughts occasionally

        Reference specific details the other person mentioned (not just the general topic)

    Avoid these anti-patterns (they make it feel like alternating lectures):

        Starting with "And then..." or "Next..." or "Now..." without referencing what
        was just said

        Introducing completely new sub-topics without connecting to previous dialogue

        Speaking as if the other person isn't there

        Using formal transitions like "Furthermore" or "Additionally"

        Every block being roughly the same length (vary it naturally)

REEL SERIES STRUCTURE:

    Multi-reel organization:

        Each reel is a self-contained chapter covering one aspect of the overall topic.

        Reels should progress logically: foundational concepts -> core mechanisms -> applications/significance.

        Each reel must have a clear, descriptive title that indicates what it covers.

        Each reel should include a brief description summarizing its focus.

        Reels should be designed to work independently but also flow as a series.
   
    Reel length and pacing:
        WORD COUNT IS LAW: Do not measure in seconds. A 3-minute reel is strictly 350 to 450 words in total across all speakers. You must not exceed 450 words per reel.

        Block Count: Use exactly 6 to 10 dialogue blocks per reel.

        Sentence Limits: Each dialogue block must be exactly 2 to 5 conversational sentences. NEVER exceed 5 sentences in a single block.

        Pacing Variety: * Fast exchanges: 2-sentence blocks of quick reactions.

            Explanations: 4-sentence blocks of deeper content.


    Content distribution across reels:

        Don't repeat information between reels - each should introduce new concepts.

        Early reels establish foundations that later reels build upon.

        The final reel should tie everything together or show real-world significance.

        Avoid making any single reel feel incomplete or rushed.

        With 3 minutes per reel, you have space to explore concepts in depth.

    Continuity between reels:

        You may briefly reference concepts from previous reels ("Remember when we talked about...").

        Don't assume the viewer watched previous reels in sequence - keep each somewhat independent.

        The first block of each reel can set context without lengthy recaps.

AVATAR BEHAVIOR:

    Avatar usage:

        Each block must use one of the provided avatars.

        Respect the avatar's speaking style and personality.

        Avatars must feel distinct.

        Let personality show through reactions, not just speech patterns.

        Maintain consistent avatar personalities across all reels in the series.

        Allow characters to have longer speaking turns when explaining complex concepts.

    Dialogue text rules:

        Plain conversational text only.

        Don't use exclamation marks (!)

        No meta comments, disclaimers, narrator text, or system talk.

        No line breaks inside a block.

        Never mention being an AI or model.

        Do not use em-dashes between words.

        Use numbers instead of Roman numerals.

        Use contractions naturally (it's, that's, we're, etc.).

SONG BLOCKS:

    Song Block Usage & Limits:

        Maximum of ONE SongBlock per reel.

        Information Over Poetry: Do NOT focus heavily on making the text rhyme perfectly or sound like a traditional pop song. Focus strictly on maximizing the educational information, facts, and explanations. The music generation engine will automatically format any text to sound like a song.

        Length Constraint: The text field must be highly detailed and contain between 3000 and 3500 characters. Do not make it shorter than 3000 characters.

MEDIA RULES:

    Use media to support understanding:
    Image Quantity: Include 6-12 image_media items per reel (scaled for 3-minute content).
    Manim Quantity & Usage:
    Strict Usage Condition: Only use manim_media if the topic is technical and requires mathematical, geometric, or physics-based visualization. If the topic is non-technical, do NOT use Manim.
    Limit: If used, include 1-4 Manim videos maximum per reel.

    Media Description:
    Images: You MUST provide exactly ONE natural sentence describing the scene. 
    CRITICAL IMAGE RULES: 
    1. Limit your description to strictly UNDER 10 WORDS.
    2. You must END the description with a period.
    3. NEVER use commas. 
    4. NEVER repeat the same word twice. 
    Example of correct output: "A messy office desk with scattered papers."
    STRICT IMAGE BAN: DO NOT output comma-separated lists or endless strings of adjectives. DO NOT use photography terms or rendering buzzwords (e.g., NO "cinematic lighting", "sharp focus", "masterpiece",   "4k"). 
    Manim: Write a clear, conceptual description of the educational animation (up to 5-10 sentences). Act   like a teacher describing a visual aid to a student. Focus on what shapes, numbers, or graphs appear, how they interact, and what metaphor they represent (e.g., "Show a 2D bar graph representing an array.  Highlight the first two bars in red, compare their heights, and swap them. Repeat this to show the largest value bubbling to the top.").
    STRICT MANIM BAN: DO NOT output any lists of keywords. DO NOT use 3D rendering terms, camera lenses,    lighting descriptions, or aesthetic buzzwords (e.g., NO "4k", "unreal engine", "ray tracing", "cinematic   lighting", "octane render", "depth of field", "motion blur"). Describe the educational visual logic only.

    Media trigger timing requirements:
    Minimum trigger length: 15 words.
    Recommended trigger length:
    Images/LaTeX: 20-40 words.
    Manim (Video): 40-60 words. Since Manim videos can be complex and long, they require a longer duration of spoken text to fully play out.
    Maximum trigger length: 60 words (Use the full 60 words for Manim triggers where possible).
    The trigger should span the portion of text where the concept is being explained.
    If a dialogue block is shorter than 15 words, do NOT include media in that block.

    Word Index Clarification:
    The start_word_index and end_word_index refer ONLY to the words inside the text field
    Word counting restarts at 0 for each block.
    Every media item MUST include a trigger with those indices.
    The 'trigger' field is REQUIRED for all media objects.
    Count carefully to ensure the trigger spans at least 15 words.
    Use inclusive counting (if words 0-14 are selected, that's 15 words).

    Media relevance:
    Media must be directly tied to the text being spoken during the trigger window.
    Don't show a visual before or after the concept is actually being discussed.
    The trigger window should cover the key explanatory sentences.

    Avatar restrictions:
    Only use the provided avatars.
    Do not invent new avatars or rename existing ones.

    Media Coexistence Rule (Image, LaTeX, Manim):
    A block may contain image_media, latex_media, manim_media, or none.
    Strict Non-Overlap: If multiple media types appear in the same block, their triggers must NOT overlap.
    You cannot display a Manim video at the same time as a static Image or LaTeX equation.
    In longer blocks, you can have multiple distinct media items as long as their triggers don't overlap.

    Media distribution strategy:
    Hierarchy:
    High Priority (Technical): Use manim_media for the deepest technical concepts. These should be the "centerpiece" visuals of the reel.
    Standard: Use image_media for general illustration and context.
    Pacing:
    Quick reaction blocks (1-2 sentences) should be pure dialogue.
    Space out media appearances so they don't feel overwhelming.
    If using a long/complex Manim video, allow a "breathing room" of pure dialogue or simple images before the next heavy animation.
